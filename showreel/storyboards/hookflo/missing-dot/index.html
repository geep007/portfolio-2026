The Missing Dot.
Seven states, one object.

1920×1080 · 30fps · 20s · no voiceover · no photography. Frames below are designed at full size and scaled; they are the source of truth for composition, scale, typography, negative space and end states in films/hookflo/MissingDot.tsx. Rev 2 incorporates the self-critique in Section 4.

Section 1 · Source

What the film is answerable to.

Full reading in brands/hookflo/source-study.html. Five things carried into this piece: the mark is a grid with a hole in it; product UI is shown running; one light, overhead; brightness is hierarchy; semantic colour only at chip scale.

Section 2 · Creative direction

The Missing Dot.

Concept
The mark is a 3×3 dot field with one position empty and one dot white — a stream of deliveries in which one is missing and one is caught. The film is a single continuous transform between that mark and a live event log.
Product truth
The event that does not arrive is the one that matters. Hookflo makes the absence visible and puts it where a human is already looking.
Motion thesis
One geometry, seven states. Nothing is created or destroyed on screen. Dots become rows, a row becomes a gap, a gap becomes a red dot, the dot routes into an alert, the alert resolves back into the row, the rows collapse into the mark.
Spatial logic
One centred panel that changes width and role. Static camera for the whole film. Nothing crosses the frame edge. The bloom never moves.
Transition logic
There are no transitions. Every apparent cut is a state change of persistent geometry. The hardest moment in the film is a full stop of motion.
Typography
Almost silent. A mono label above the panel that rewrites three times, and the site's own headline at the close. Everything between is UI type doing a UI job.
Density arc
0.08 → 0.55 → 0.72 → 0.28 → 0.50 → 0.45 → 0.12. The film's widest moment is the failure; its emptiest is the detection.
Forbidden
Camera moves · off-frame entrances · fade-in · springs · kinetic type · dramatising the failure at the instant it occurs · particles · node graphs · glow on objects · a second serif.

Section 4 · Self-critique · rev 1 → rev 2

What was wrong with the first pass.

Rev 1 was rendered at full size and inspected as static design. Seven problems, all of them the same problem: the frames had the brand's colours and type but were behaving like a diagram, not like Hookflo. Fixed frames are what you see above.

C1
The log panel was 748px tall holding 544px of content. 200px of dead air inside a container reads as an unfinished layout, not as negative space — and this brand's negative space is always outside containers.
fixPanel to 1200×620, rows to 60px, eight of them. Content now fills the panel and the breathing room moved outside it, where the brand keeps it.
C2
No mono label above the panel — a direct violation of the brand's own rule R7, and it left the top sixth of every frame empty. It also meant the film had no way to speak without narrating.
fixAn uppercase mono label above the panel that rewrites three times: Live deliveries → webhook.failed → Routing to channel. That is now the film's entire voice, and it is the site's own device.
C3
Source names were bare text. On the site a source is always paired with its mark; without them the rows read as an abstract diagram of a log rather than as a log. This was the biggest generic risk in the piece.
fix28px monogram tiles in each service's own colour at low saturation — st ck sb gh — borrowing the ecosystem without making it the hero.
C4
The scan line was misaligned with the row it stops on, and ran the full frame width. It is the single most brand-specific gesture in the film and it was sloppy.
fixAligned to the failing row's centre and clipped to the panel's inner width. The scan happens inside the instrument, because that is where Hookflo is looking.
C5
The route path was drawn straight across the dimmed rows. It read as a rendering mistake, and it broke rule R1 by treating the panel as if it were not a container.
fixThe path now begins at the panel's right edge at the failing row's height and lives entirely in the gutter between the two panels. The dot leaves the container properly, and the gutter — previously empty — becomes the place where routing is legible.
C6
K5's bottom-right quadrant was a void with nothing placed in it. Unplaced voids are the difference between asymmetry and imbalance.
fixA mono delivery receipt under the card — delivered to #eng-alerts 1.4s after failure — which weights the corner and is also the film's proof of the actual product claim.
C7
K7 had mark, wordmark and headline at three competing scales, all starting from the same x with no structure between them. It looked like an end card rather than like this brand.
fixRestacked as the site does it: small lockup above, 108px headline below, both on the 300px margin, right half left empty. It is now recognisably the hookflo.com hero, rebuilt in the film's own geometry.
C8
Nothing carried a running count, so the DROP beat's central claim — the system does not know yet — was invisible in a still.
fixA mono counter under the panel: 12 delivered · 0 failed during the drop, 1 failed after detection, 1 alerted after resolution. Three words carry the entire product argument.

What was checked and left alone: the panel stays dead-centre for K2/K3/K4/K6. It is the most symmetrical choice available and normally a warning sign, but here the panel is one persistent object being re-read — moving it between states would break the film's only structural idea. The asymmetry lives where the brand puts it: inside the container in K5, and against the margin in K1 and K7.

Section 5 · Spatial continuity

One object, seven readings.

The band is the persistent log panel: width and x-position over time. The line is the tracked dot — the same object from the mark's centre through the failing row, the route, the alert card and back.

Section 6 · Motion notes

Why each thing moves.

Cadence, not stagger

Rows arrive every 12 frames exactly. A designer's stagger implies a person placed them; an interval implies a machine emitting them. The interval breaks in exactly one place — the drop misses a single beat.

The failure is not announced

No flash, no shake, no push-in at the moment of the drop. The row is blank and the film carries on over it for 60 frames. If the film reacted, it would be claiming the problem is visible — the opposite of the product.

The hard stop

The most important cut is not a cut: at 0:09.0 all motion ceases in one frame and every row drops to 12%. Everything after that is Hookflo looking, and the change of agent is felt without being stated.

Scan is linear

The reading head moves at constant speed with no ease at either end, straight from the site's scroll-linked paragraph. An eased head reads as a spotlight; a constant one reads as a process.

One travel only

Exactly one object crosses the frame in 20 seconds: the red dot, on a right-angled path, at cubic-in-out, entirely within the gutter. Because nothing else has ever travelled, it does not need to be big.

Brightness is the edit

Nothing fades in. Brightness rises within a state and steps down only at a state boundary. Every reveal is a change of attention over material that was already there.

The clock never resets

03:14:00 → 03:14:17, running under everything including the stillness. Proof that the world did not pause between beats, and what makes seven states one continuous event.

Three holds

40 frames on the mark, 34 on the lit failing row, 24 on the final lockup. Nothing moves in any of them. These are the only moments the composition can be read, and they carry the piece's statements.

The accent budget

Lavender marks one thing per state: the mark, the scan head, the alerted chip, the second headline line. Red exists for 5.4 seconds and never occupies more than 15px until the alert card. Green is always chip-scale.
